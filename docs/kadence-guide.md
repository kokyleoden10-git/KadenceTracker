# Kadence — my guide

Why this app exists, and how I actually want to use it.

## Why I'm rebuilding this

Kadence v1 turned into a journal instead of a tracker. I was logging individual events instead of habits, and I'd built a huge library of activity-to-context tags that took more energy to maintain than the habits themselves. It felt like it could spiral, so I stopped using it.

This version is smaller on purpose. Five kinds of things to track, four areas of life, tags I don't have to think about. If it gets complicated again, that's a sign to cut, not add.

## The four areas

- **Wellbeing** — sleep, meditation, tarot, the stuff I'm trying to drink or smoke less of
- **Knowledge** — reading, Japanese, anything I'm learning
- **Creativity** — DJing, drawing, music discovery, making things
- **Systems** — chores, scheduling, money, the infrastructure that keeps the other three running

Not every area needs attention every day. Some days lean toward one thing, other days another. That's the point.

## Two kinds of habits

**Anchors** are small and daily. Two minutes, tops. Meditate for two minutes, make the bed, whatever's tiny enough that skipping it isn't really an option.

**Practices** are deeper and don't happen every day. DJing practice, a real reading session, a Japanese lesson that isn't just app taps. I pick which days these show up on, and I can change that whenever my week changes.

## Readings get their own space

Tarot pulls and astrology aren't generic log entries anymore, they're their own thing, because they're the habit I've actually stuck with the longest. Deck, spread, the cards, my notes. If I ever want to see whether a reading correlates with how a day actually goes, the data's already sitting there in a shape that supports that. Not forcing the correlation, just leaving the door open.

## Tags, kept small

Three tags max per entry. Type `Japanese` or `japanese`, doesn't matter, the app treats them as the same tag and shows me what already exists so I'm not building five versions of the same thing by accident. No dropdown menus of pre-built contexts to fill out. If a tag doesn't exist yet, I just type it.

## Streaks stay, judgment doesn't

I like streaks, they keep me honest. But a broken streak is one data point, not a verdict on the week. The weekly reflection is where I actually ask what happened, not just whether I hit the number.

## The weekly reflection

Three questions, every week:

1. What went well?
2. What broke, and what happened right before?
3. Any pattern I noticed, between a Signal and a Reading, or across domains?

That third question is where the actual insight happens. Daily logging is just collecting the data.

## Signals

Sleep, HRV, recovery, whatever PeakWatch or the Apple Watch is telling me. Manual entry for now, syncing the APIs directly can come later once the basic habit is solid.

## Privacy

This stays on my device. No cloud analytics, no data going anywhere I haven't explicitly sent it. Entries about drinking or smoking get a quieter privacy tag by default, so if I ever build sharing or export features, those stay out unless I say otherwise.

## Ideas for later, not now

Trend charts, AI-generated summaries of my reflections, syncing wearables automatically, the astrology-correlation thing done properly. All genuinely interesting. All explicitly not week one. The whole reason this is possible to add later without a rebuild is that the data underneath is being kept honest and simple from the start.

## The actual philosophy, if I need reminding

The app's job is to hand me back a story about my own life, not to grade me on one. Small on the days that need small, deeper on the days that can hold more. If using it ever starts to feel like the chore itself, that's the system's fault, not mine, and it's a sign to simplify rather than push through.
